E Source Resume Screener

An AI screener I built for E Source's hiring team. It reads every resume against the job, scores it with the evidence attached, and sorts the pile so people spend their time on the right candidates.

Built
Fall 2025
For
The hiring team at E Source
Built with
React, Supabase and Claude

The dashboard, and one candidate's evaluation. Shown with sample candidates, not real applicants.

The problem

The first pass on a stack of resumes is slow, and it's hard to hold the same bar on the fiftieth resume as on the first. Keyword filters are fast but blunt, and they can't tell you why someone is a fit.

Why I built it

I spend a lot of time on the hiring side of the table, so this one was personal. E Source needed a faster first pass that their team could still trust, and I wanted to see how far Claude could take it if every score had to show its work.

Every score shows its work.

What it does

  • Upload resumes in bulk: PDF, Word, plain text, or even an image of one.
  • Claude scores each candidate from 0 to 100, with subscores for technical skills, experience, education and cultural fit, plus a recommendation: phone screen, needs review, or reject.
  • A skills table lists each required skill, how well it matches, and the line from the resume behind it.
  • Strengths, gaps and red flags, including a flag for applications that look AI-generated.
  • Bias detection, an option to anonymize the evaluation, and an evaluation prompt and reject threshold the team can tune themselves. Everything exports to CSV.

What it's changed for me

It sharpened a view I hold pretty strongly: the answer isn't ban AI, but the answer also isn't AI without human review. The screener does the first read and shows its evidence, and the team decides how much to trust it, down to the reject threshold.

It also showed me that the evidence matters more than the score. A number on its own gets argued with. A number with the line from the resume behind it gets used.
