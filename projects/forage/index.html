The problem

Good stuff was everywhere, and none of it was in one place. Newsletters sat in Gmail, videos in YouTube, books in StoryGraph, and everything else in open tabs. So I'd pick whatever was in front of me instead of what I actually wanted to spend time on.

Why I built it

I started Forage in March to fix the inbox half. It pulled newsletters, articles and videos into one feed, gave me a short read on each, and sent one email at 8pm so I didn't have to open forty tabs.

In September it grew into what it is now. The question I care about most is "what should I pick up next?", so Forage became a library first: books, films, TV, music, podcasts, newsletters, YouTube and articles, all in one place, with discovery built in. The reading feed is still there, it's just not the center anymore.

Intention over drift.

What it does

  • Every morning, Discover adds a fresh batch of picks grounded in what I've loved, and each one shows its reason, so I can see why it's there.
  • The Library holds what's up next, my shelves and my favorites, across eight kinds of media.
  • Through the day it syncs Gmail, RSS and YouTube, and the 8pm email rolls up the day's reading.
  • Separate watch lists for what I watch on my own and what we watch as a family.
  • A public page with my top tens, which is where the favorites on this site point.
  • It installs as an app on my phone, and a Chrome extension saves anything I find along the way.

What it's changed for me

I open it every day. The bar I hold it to is the same one from day one: am I choosing what to read, watch or listen to next on purpose, or am I drifting? Having one place, with the reason attached to every pick, makes on purpose the easy default.

It's also where I play. It's been 266 commits since March, and plenty of them are tiny fixes, which is what happens when you use the thing you build every day. The engraved look took four rounds of comps before it felt right, and I'd do it again.

If you're curious, my favorites are public, and I wrote a short launch post on LinkedIn.
